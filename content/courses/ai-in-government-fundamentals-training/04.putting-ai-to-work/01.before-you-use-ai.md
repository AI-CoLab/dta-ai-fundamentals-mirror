# Before you use AI

::process
:::step{kind="intro"}
### Before you use AI

Before you even begin to use AI at work, ask yourself a few easy questions.
:::

:::step{kind="summary"}
If there’s not an easy or clear direction, use AI ethics frameworks such as [Australia’s AI Ethics Principles](https://architecture.digital.gov.au/australias-artificial-intelligence-ethics-principles-0) to guide your choices in a safe, responsible way.
:::

:::step{kind="step"}
### What am I trying to achieve?

Keep the requirements and ideal outcome of your task in mind to decide which:

- tools to use
- stakeholders to involve
- processes to follow.
:::

:::step{kind="step"}
### Would it be appropriate to use AI?

Some general-use tools, like generative AI, hold unique risks in policy and service delivery.

Alongside AI ethics frameworks, consider how you will manage prompts and outputs for:

- **accuracy** – how will you verify that information is accurate and relevant?

- **bias –** how will you identify and counter biases in prompts and outputs?

- **sensitivity** – how will you prevent, identify or remediate the use of personal, classified, sensitive or copyright-protected information?

- **transparency** – how will you make it clear where AI has influenced your advice or decisions, and allow them to be contested?
:::

:::step{kind="step"}
### What do I need to know before starting?

Not every choice will be easy to decide, so familiarise yourself with:

- your agency’s own AI and ICT use policies

- whole-of-government guidance, such as the Australian Government's [Guidance for using generative AI](https://architecture.digital.gov.au/guidance-generative-ai#:~:text=Inputs%20into%20public%20generative%20AI,the%20Protective%20Security%20Policy%20Framework\).)

- your workplaces' values, code of conduct and other requirements.
:::
