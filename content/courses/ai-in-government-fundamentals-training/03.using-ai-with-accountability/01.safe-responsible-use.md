# Safe, responsible use

::callout{style="c"}
The community expects government to be an exemplar of the responsible use of AI.
::

People who work in government should have the confidence and support to make responsible choices about using AI.

Many resources about existing technology also apply to AI, like your agency's ICT use policies, values and code of conduct. But some decisions will not have an easy or clear direction.

::callout{style="b"}
AI Ethics frameworks such as [Australia's AI Ethics Principles](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/australias-artificial-intelligence-ethics-framework/australias-ai-ethics-principles), can guide your choices about using AI to:
::

- benefit individuals, society and the environment and ensure its not inaccurate or misleading
- respect human rights, diversity and the autonomy of individuals
- be inclusive and accessible, and ensure it does not involve or result in unfair discrimination against individuals, communities or groups
- uphold privacy rights, data protection and data security laws.

::continue

::knowledge-check{kind="matching"}
Drag each principle to the action you should take to uphold it.

- Accountability
  - You should be able to explain, justify and take ownership of your advice and decisions.
- Transparency and explainability
  - You should be transparent about where and how you use AI in your work.
- Human-centred values
  - Only use outputs that respect human rights, diversity and the autonomy of individuals.
- Contestability
  - Identify how people can challenge the use of AI or its outcomes in your work.
- Reliability and safety
  - Only use AI when it reliably performs its intended purpose.
- Fairness
  - Make sure outputs are fair and meet community expectations before using them.
- Human, societal and environmental wellbeing
  - Consider how using AI will benefit individuals, society and the environment.
- Privacy protection and security
  - Only use AI in a way that meets your data privacy, protection and security obligations.
